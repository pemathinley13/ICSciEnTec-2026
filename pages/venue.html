About Bhutan

Where Tradition Meets The Himalayas

A Kingdom Unlike Any Other

Tucked between India and Tibet, Bhutan is one of the world's most tightly guarded secrets — a carbon-negative kingdom that measures progress not only in GDP, but in Gross National Happiness. Whitewashed dzongs, saffron-robed monks and terraced rice fields sit beneath snow-capped Himalayan peaks that have never been mapped by climbers.

For delegates of ICSciEnTec 2027, Bhutan offers more than a backdrop — it offers a rare chance to experience a living culture that has protected its forests, its architecture and its identity while quietly becoming a hub for renewable energy, engineering and technology research in the Eastern Himalayas.

College of Science and Technology (CST) · Rinchending, Phuentsholing

About the Venue

The College of Science and Technology (CST) is a constituent college of the Royal University of Bhutan (RUB) and the country's premier institution for engineering and technology education. First established in 1972 as the Royal Bhutan Polytechnic, the college moved to its present campus at Rinchending in 2000 and became the first institution in Bhutan to offer Bachelor's degrees, in Civil and Electrical Engineering. It has since grown into a full-fledged college offering undergraduate and Master's programmes across engineering, architecture and construction management.

Getting There

By air: Fly into Paro International Airport, Bhutan's only international gateway, then travel onward by road to Phuentsholing.

By land: Phuentsholing sits directly on the Bhutan–India border, adjoining the Indian town of Jaigaon (West Bengal) — a common overland route for delegates travelling from India, Bangladesh and Nepal.

Visa and permit requirements vary by nationality — please confirm current rules before booking travel.
